Bilal ibn Rabah was an enslaved Abyssinian man in Makkah when he accepted Islam, and his owner, Umayyah ibn Khalaf, tortured him mercilessly for it - dragging him across the scorching desert sand and placing a massive rock on his chest, demanding he renounce his faith and worship idols instead. Through it all, Bilal repeated only one word: "Ahad, Ahad" - One, One, referring to the oneness of Allah.

Abu Bakr, moved by his suffering, purchased Bilal from his tormentor and freed him immediately, asking nothing in return. Bilal remained close to the Prophet ever since, and when the call to prayer was instituted in Madinah, the Prophet chose Bilal's voice to be the very first Adhan ever called, making him the first muezzin in Islamic history.

Bilal's voice called the faithful to prayer for years, but after the Prophet's death, he found himself unable to complete the call without breaking into tears at the memory of him, and largely stopped calling the Adhan in Madinah out of sheer grief. He is remembered as living proof that Islam values a person's faith and character over lineage or worldly status, and the Prophet himself once told him he heard the sound of his footsteps ahead of him in Paradise.
