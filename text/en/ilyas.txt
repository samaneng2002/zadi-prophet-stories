Ilyas (Elijah, peace be upon him) was sent to his people at a time when they had turned to worshipping an idol named Ba'l instead of Allah. He confronted them directly, asking how they could call upon a lifeless idol they had carved themselves while abandoning the Best of Creators, who alone had given them their sustenance and their very existence.

His people refused his call and continued in their idolatry, choosing to be brought later before punishment rather than turn back from what their forefathers had practiced. The Quran honors Ilyas among the righteous and among those granted peace by later generations, describing him as one of the Messengers sent to guide a community that ultimately chose to reject the truth despite clear warning.

Though the Quran gives only a brief account of his mission compared to some other Prophets, Ilyas's story shares the same essential message carried by every Prophet before and after him: that worship belongs to Allah alone, and that turning away from ancestral custom toward truth - however difficult that may be - is always worth the cost.
