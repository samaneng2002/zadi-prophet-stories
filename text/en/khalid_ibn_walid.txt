Khalid ibn Al-Walid was one of Makkah's most brilliant military commanders while still an opponent of Islam, and it was his tactical skill that handed the Muslims their only real defeat at the Battle of Uhud, exploiting a gap left by archers who abandoned their post too soon. For years he remained one of Islam's most capable adversaries.

He eventually accepted Islam himself, traveling to Madinah specifically to embrace it and seek the Prophet's forgiveness for his past opposition. The Prophet welcomed him warmly and, recognizing his exceptional skill, soon placed him in command of Muslim forces. After a difficult, evenly matched battle at Mu'tah where three appointed commanders were killed in succession, Khalid took charge and executed a skillful, orderly withdrawal that saved the Muslim army from destruction - an achievement so remarkable that the Prophet gave him the title "Sayfullah," the Sword of Allah.

Khalid went on to lead Muslim armies to a remarkable, unbroken string of victories across Arabia, Persia, and the Levant, becoming one of history's most celebrated undefeated generals. Despite his enormous military success, he remained famously humble about it, once telling his soldiers before a battle that he hoped to die a martyr's death in bed rather than take credit for his victories - a wish that, in the end, was not granted, as he died peacefully of illness, still mourning that he had not been killed in battle for Allah's cause.
