Bal'am ibn Ba'ura was a man among the Children of Israel who had once been given great knowledge - some accounts say he knew Allah's Greatest Name, the supplication by which any prayer is answered. He was, by all early signs, a righteous scholar whose knowledge could have raised him to a noble station.

When Musa (Moses) approached with his people to confront a tyrant king, that king begged Bal'am to pray against Musa, offering him wealth and status. Bal'am knew Musa was a true Prophet and refused at first, but the king kept pressing him, and gradually Bal'am's love for worldly gain overcame what he knew to be right. He gave in and turned his knowledge against the very truth he recognized.

The Quran describes his fate starkly: he "clung to the earth and followed his own desire," likened to a dog that pants whether you drive it away or leave it alone - restless and never satisfied. His story is a warning that knowledge alone does not save a person; it is what one does with it, and the pull of this world can corrupt even someone who was once given real insight into the truth.
