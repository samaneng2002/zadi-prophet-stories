Two men were given as companions - one owned two lush, thriving gardens surrounded by date palms, with a stream flowing between them and abundant fruit; the other was comparatively poor. The wealthy man grew proud of what he had, walking through his gardens boasting that it would never perish, and doubting the Hour would ever come at all.

His companion reminded him gently that everything he had was a gift from Allah, not his own doing, and that he ought to say "as Allah wills, there is no power except through Allah" rather than take pride in it. He warned that Allah could easily send a calamity that would destroy it all in a single night - but the wealthy man, secure in his arrogance, paid no attention.

That very calamity came to pass: the gardens were struck and utterly ruined, their fruit destroyed, leaving the man wringing his hands in regret over everything he had spent building it, admitting too late that he should never have associated anyone or anything with his Lord. The story is a lasting reminder that wealth and blessings are a trust from Allah, never a guarantee, and that gratitude - not pride - is what preserves them.
