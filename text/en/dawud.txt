Dawud (David, peace be upon him) rose from the ranks of an ordinary soldier to become a king, after showing extraordinary courage in single combat against the fearsome Jalut (Goliath), whom he defeated with a simple sling when seasoned warriors had hesitated. Allah granted him both prophethood and kingship together, a rare combination among the Prophets, along with a voice so beautiful that mountains and birds would join him in glorifying Allah when he recited.

Allah also softened iron for Dawud, allowing him to shape it with his bare hands as easily as clay, and he used this gift to craft coats of armor - earning his own living through his own skilled work rather than relying solely on the royal treasury, a detail the Quran highlights as a model of honest labor even for a king.

Dawud was known for his justice as a ruler and his intense devotion in worship, famously dividing his nights between sleep, prayer, and rest, and fasting every other day - a pattern the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ later described as the most beloved form of voluntary worship to Allah. His life stands as an example of combining worldly responsibility with deep, personal devotion.
