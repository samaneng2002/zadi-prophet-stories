Hud (peace be upon him) was sent to the people of 'Aad, a powerful nation known for their great strength and remarkable buildings raised on high ground - people so proud of their might that they believed nothing could ever overpower them. Hud called them to worship Allah alone and to give up the idols they had inherited from their forefathers, warning them that strength without gratitude to their Creator would not save them.

His people mocked him, accusing him of foolishness and daring him to bring about the punishment he warned of if he were truthful. Hud reminded them of the blessings Allah had given them - strength, cattle, sons, gardens and springs - and pleaded with them not to spread corruption on the earth. Still, only a small number believed him.

When the punishment finally came, it arrived as a fierce, roaring wind that raged over them for days, uprooting them as though they were hollow trunks of palm trees. Hud and the believers with him were saved, while the 'Aad who had trusted in their own might were destroyed - a lasting lesson that no strength stands against the will of Allah.
