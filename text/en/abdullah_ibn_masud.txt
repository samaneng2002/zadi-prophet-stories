Abdullah ibn Mas'ud was a poor shepherd boy in Makkah, tending another man's flock, when he first encountered the Prophet Muhammad and Abu Bakr, who asked if they might drink some of the sheep's milk. Ibn Mas'ud explained the sheep were not his to give, and impressed by his honesty even as a boy with almost nothing, the Prophet asked for a young, barren sheep instead and, in a small miracle, milked it himself when it should have produced none at all.

He soon accepted Islam, becoming one of the earliest converts, and grew unusually close to the Prophet, serving him personally and memorizing the Quran with such precision that the Prophet himself said, "Whoever wishes to recite the Quran fresh as it was revealed, let him recite it in the manner of Ibn Umm Abd," using his mother's name as was the custom of honor. He was the first person in Islamic history to recite the Quran openly and defiantly before the hostile chiefs of Quraysh at the Ka'bah, at great personal risk.

After the Prophet's death, Ibn Mas'ud became one of the foremost teachers of Quran and Islamic law in the growing Muslim empire, sent to Kufa where he trained a whole generation of scholars who carried his teaching forward for centuries. Despite his physical smallness - he was known for having thin legs - the Prophet once remarked that his legs would weigh heavier than Mount Uhud on the Day of Judgment, a testament to how much his knowledge and devotion outweighed his humble appearance.
