Harut and Marut were two angels sent down to Babylon at a time when people had begun practicing magic and attributing it to the Prophet Sulayman, falsely accusing him of sorcery. Allah sent the two angels as a trial for mankind, and they would openly warn anyone who came to learn magic from them: "We are only a trial, so do not disbelieve."

Despite the warning, many people ignored it and sought to learn magic anyway, drawn by the harm and division it could cause between a man and his wife. Some of those who came were sincere seekers led astray; others were already corrupt, using the two angels' knowledge for evil ends they had chosen for themselves.

The Quran is clear that magic can only ever harm by Allah's permission, and that whoever trades away the Hereafter for it has made the worse bargain. The story stands as a warning that knowledge itself is not evil - what matters is the intention behind seeking it, and magic sought for harm was always destined to fail its user in the end.
