Dhul-Kifl (peace be upon him) is mentioned in the Quran only briefly, alongside Prophets such as Ismail and Idris, described as being among the patient and among the righteous. His name means "the one who was given a pledge" or "the one with a double portion," and scholars have differed over the details of his life and even his exact identity, since the Quran does not narrate his story at length the way it does for some other Prophets.

What is agreed upon is the character the Quran highlights in him: patience under difficulty, and steadfastness in righteousness. Some accounts describe him as a man who took on a heavy responsibility - committing to judge among his people with justice, to fast by day and pray by night, and to never lose his temper - and who fulfilled that pledge faithfully throughout his life, earning Allah's praise for it.

Dhul-Kifl's brief mention teaches an important lesson in itself: that a person's honor before Allah does not depend on how much of their story reaches later generations, but on the sincerity and consistency of their devotion, known fully only to their Creator.
