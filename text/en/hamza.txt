Hamza ibn Abdul-Muttalib was the Prophet Muhammad's own paternal uncle and, remarkably, close to him in age, having grown up together almost like brothers. Known throughout Makkah as a formidable, fearless warrior and hunter, his acceptance of Islam came suddenly and dramatically: after hearing that Abu Jahl had insulted and struck the Prophet in the street, Hamza confronted him publicly and struck him in return, declaring his own new faith on the spot in open defiance of the whole tribe.

His conversion gave the small Muslim community real protection, since few in Makkah dared provoke a man of his strength and standing. He went on to fight in the early battles of Islam, most notably at Badr, where his courage helped turn the tide for the vastly outnumbered Muslim army.

At the Battle of Uhud, Hamza fought fiercely once again, but was ambushed and killed by a spear thrown by Wahshi ibn Harb, a slave promised his freedom for the act by Hind bint Utbah, whose own relatives had died at Badr. His body was then brutally mutilated in revenge, a sight that grieved the Prophet deeply. He was given the title "Asadullah" - the Lion of Allah - and "Sayyid ash-Shuhada," the Master of the Martyrs, a title he holds to this day.
