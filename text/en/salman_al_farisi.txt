Salman Al-Farisi began life in Persia as the son of a Zoroastrian nobleman, but grew dissatisfied with his family's religion and set off on an extraordinary personal search for the truth, attaching himself to a series of Christian monks across the region, one after another, as each in turn passed away or directed him further. The last of them told him a Prophet would soon appear in Arabia, bearing certain unmistakable signs, and urged him to seek him out.

His journey there was anything but easy: he was tricked and sold into slavery along the way, ending up owned by a Jewish man in Madinah. It was there, still enslaved, that he finally heard of Muhammad's arrival, and secretly went to test the very signs he had been told to look for - including the seal of prophethood between his shoulders. Recognizing it, he wept and immediately accepted Islam.

The Prophet helped him negotiate and pay off his freedom soon after. Salman went on to make one of the most consequential contributions of the early Muslim community: during the Battle of the Trench, he suggested digging a deep trench around Madinah, a defensive tactic entirely unknown to the Arabs but standard in Persia, which successfully protected the city from a massive combined siege. The Prophet honored him by declaring, "Salman is from us, the people of the household" - a testament to how completely his long search for truth had been rewarded.
