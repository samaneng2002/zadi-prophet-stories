Fatimah Az-Zahra was the youngest daughter of the Prophet Muhammad and Khadijah, and remained the only one of his children to outlive him, becoming an especially close companion to her father in his final years in Madinah. The Prophet loved her so deeply that he would stand up to greet her whenever she entered a room, and would say that she was a part of him - "whoever angers her, angers me."

She married Ali ibn Abi Talib, and their household, though materially simple and often short on comforts, became the source of the Prophet's own beloved grandchildren, Hasan and Husayn, along with Zaynab and Umm Kulthum. Despite being the Prophet's daughter, she lived a life of real hardship, grinding grain and carrying water herself until her hands bore the marks of it, once asking her father for a servant to ease the burden.

Instead of granting her a servant, the Prophet taught her and Ali a form of remembrance to recite before sleep - glorifying, praising, and declaring Allah's greatness a set number of times each - telling her it was better for her than a servant. She passed away only months after her father, and is remembered as "Sayyidat Nisa' Ahl al-Jannah," the Leader of the Women of Paradise, a title the Prophet himself gave her.
