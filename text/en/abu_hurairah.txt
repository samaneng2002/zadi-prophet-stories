Abu Hurairah accepted Islam relatively late, arriving in Madinah only a few years before the Prophet's death, yet he became the single most prolific narrator of the Prophet's sayings in all of Islamic history - a fact he attributed to a very particular circumstance rather than any special gift.

Unlike many companions who were busy with trade, farming, or family responsibilities, Abu Hurairah was poor and often hungry, and chose to spend nearly all his time attached to the Prophet's own company, attending every gathering and memorizing everything he heard, simply because he had nothing else pulling him away. He once told others that his fellow companions were occupied with their business in the markets while he stayed close to the Prophet purely "for the sake of his stomach," meaning he had no distractions of wealth to manage.

He asked the Prophet directly to pray that he would never forget what he learned, and the Prophet did so, spreading his cloak for Abu Hurairah to gather knowledge into it, so to speak, before folding it against his own chest. From that day, he said, he never forgot a single narration again. He went on to teach extensively in Madinah after the Prophet's death, and to this day his name appears attached to more preserved hadith than any other companion.
