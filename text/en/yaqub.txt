Yaqub (Jacob, peace be upon him), son of Ishaq and grandson of Ibrahim, was a Prophet blessed with twelve sons, the most beloved of whom was Yusuf. When Yusuf shared a dream of eleven stars, the sun, and the moon prostrating before him, Yaqub recognized its meaning at once and gently warned him not to tell his brothers, fearing their jealousy.

When Yusuf was lost to him - taken by his own brothers and later brought to his father as a false story of a wolf's attack, complete with a shirt stained in blood that did not match a genuine attack - Yaqub did not fully believe them, sensing something was wrong, though he could prove nothing. He said only, "So patience is most fitting," and turned his grief to Allah alone, weeping until his eyesight weakened, yet never losing hope even after decades of separation.

When his other sons pressed him to stop mentioning Yusuf, he told them he complained of his sorrow and grief only to Allah, and that he knew from Allah what they did not know. His patience was finally rewarded when Yusuf's shirt was sent to restore his sight, and the family was reunited in Egypt - a story of a father's unbroken faith standing at the very heart of the account of Yusuf.
