Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas accepted Islam while still a young man, among the earliest converts, and became renowned as the first person in Islamic history to fire an arrow in defense of the faith. The Prophet held him in such high regard for his marksmanship that he once said, "Shoot, O Sa'd, my father and mother be sacrificed for you" - a phrase of praise he offered to no one else in that exact form.

His mother, deeply opposed to his conversion, once refused to eat or drink at all until he abandoned Islam, hoping to pressure him through sheer desperation. Sa'd remained firm, telling her that even if she had a hundred souls to lose, he would never leave his faith - a resolve later reflected in the Quran's own guidance that a child must still treat parents with kindness even while never obeying them into disbelief.

Sa'd went on to become one of the most important military commanders of the early Islamic conquests, most famously leading the Muslim army to victory at the decisive Battle of Al-Qadisiyyah against the Persian Empire, opening the way for Islam's spread across Persia. He was among the ten companions promised Paradise by name, and is remembered as a man whose prayers were so consistently answered that people specifically sought him out to pray on their behalf.
