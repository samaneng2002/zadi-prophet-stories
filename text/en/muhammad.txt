Muhammad (peace be upon him) was born in Makkah, orphaned of his father before birth and losing his mother at a young age, raised first by his grandfather and then his uncle Abu Talib. Even before his prophethood, he was known among his people as "Al-Amin" - the trustworthy one - for his honesty in trade and his fairness in resolving disputes.

At the age of forty, while retreating to a cave on the mountain of Hira to reflect, he received the first revelation of the Quran through the angel Jibreel, marking the beginning of twenty-three years of guidance that would reshape the world. He called the people of Makkah to abandon their idols and worship Allah alone, facing years of ridicule, boycott, and persecution alongside the small community of believers who joined him, some of whom endured severe torture for their faith.

After thirteen years of hardship in Makkah, he migrated to Madinah - an event that marks the start of the Islamic calendar - where he established a community built on justice, brotherhood, and the worship of Allah, uniting tribes that had feuded for generations. He endured several major battles in defense of the growing Muslim community, yet remained known for his mercy, forgiving even those who had once fought fiercely against him once Makkah was peacefully reclaimed.

He passed away in Madinah after completing his final pilgrimage and delivering a farewell sermon that emphasized justice, the sanctity of life and property, and the equality of all people regardless of race or lineage. He is remembered as the final Messenger of Allah, sent as a mercy to all of creation, whose example in worship, character, and daily conduct remains the model Muslims strive to follow to this day.
