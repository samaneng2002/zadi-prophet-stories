Nuh (Noah, peace be upon him) was sent to a people who had fallen into the worship of idols named after righteous men from generations before them. He called his people to worship Allah alone for nine hundred and fifty years, patiently repeating his message day and night, in private and in public - yet only a small number of people believed him.

When it became clear his people would never accept the truth, Allah commanded Nuh to build a great ship, even though there was no sea nearby and no sign of rain. His own people mocked him for it, but he continued in obedience. When the command finally came, water burst forth from the earth and poured down from the sky, and the ship carried Nuh, the believers with him, and a pair of every kind of animal to safety, while the flood overwhelmed the rest of the land.

Even Nuh's own son refused to board the ship, trusting instead in climbing a mountain for safety, and was lost in the flood - a reminder that faith, not family alone, is what saves a person. After the flood receded, Nuh and those with him began life anew, and his story stands as the account of patience carried to its furthest limit in calling people back to their Creator.
