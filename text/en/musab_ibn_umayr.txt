Mus'ab ibn Umayr was born into one of Makkah's wealthiest families, raised in luxury and famous even among Quraysh for his fine clothing and perfume. When he secretly accepted Islam and his mother discovered it, she cut off his allowance and support entirely, and he went almost overnight from the best-dressed young man in Makkah to wearing patched, worn-out clothing - a change so visible that companions who had known him before wept to see it.

Despite losing everything materially, Mus'ab never once complained or asked to be released from his faith. The Prophet recognized his intelligence and gentle manner and sent him to Madinah before the migration as Islam's very first ambassador, tasked with teaching the Quran to the new believers there and inviting more of the city's tribes to Islam - a mission he carried out so successfully that, within a year, Islam had spread through nearly every household in Madinah, paving the way for the Prophet's own migration there.

At the Battle of Uhud, Mus'ab carried the Muslim banner and was killed defending it, his body so poor by then that the single cloth available to bury him in was too short to cover both his head and his feet at once. The Prophet, deeply moved, recalled how Mus'ab had once been the most elegantly dressed youth in Makkah, and instructed that his head be covered with the cloth and his feet covered with grass instead - a stark image of everything Mus'ab had willingly given up for his faith.
