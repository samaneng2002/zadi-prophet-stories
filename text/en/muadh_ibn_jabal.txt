Mu'adh ibn Jabal accepted Islam as a young man in Madinah and quickly became known among the companions for his deep knowledge of Islamic law, so much so that the Prophet himself once said that Mu'adh would be the most knowledgeable of his followers regarding what is lawful and unlawful. He combined that knowledge with a warmth and generosity that made him widely beloved among the community.

When the Prophet sent him to Yemen to teach Islam and serve as a judge, he asked Mu'adh how he would rule in matters brought before him. Mu'adh answered that he would judge first by the Quran, then by the Prophet's own example if the Quran did not address it directly, and if neither covered the matter, he would exercise his own reasoned judgment. The Prophet was pleased with this careful, layered answer, praising Allah for guiding His messenger's envoy to give exactly the right response.

Mu'adh remained deeply devoted throughout his life, and it was reported that some of his final words, spoken as he lay dying during a plague in the Levant, were an expression of longing to meet Allah, saying he did not fear death out of any love for this world, but simply for the sake of the thirst of long days of fasting and the standing of long nights in prayer that he would no longer be able to offer.
