Ishaq (Isaac, peace be upon him) was born to Ibrahim and his wife Sarah in their old age, a child given as glad tidings by angels who visited Ibrahim's household on their way to the people of Lut. Sarah was astonished at the news, given her advanced age, but the angels reminded her that nothing is difficult for Allah's decree.

Ishaq grew up as a Prophet in his own right, continuing his father's mission of calling people to the worship of Allah alone. He became the father of Yaqub, and through that lineage, many later Prophets - including Yusuf, Musa, Harun, Dawud, Sulayman, and Isa - trace their descent back to him, making him a central link in the chain of Prophethood after Ibrahim.

Though the Quran does not narrate a long, detailed story of Ishaq's own life as it does for some other Prophets, he is repeatedly honored alongside his father and his son as a man of righteousness, guided by Allah to do good and to establish prayer and charity - one of the quiet, steady links that carried the message of pure monotheism forward across generations.
