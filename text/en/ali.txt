Ali ibn Abi Talib was the Prophet Muhammad's own first cousin, raised partly in his household, and among the very first people ever to accept Islam - some accounts say the first child to do so. He grew up witnessing the Prophet's character firsthand, long before prophethood was even announced.

On the night the Prophet migrated to Madinah, with Makkah's chiefs surrounding his house intending to kill him, Ali slept in the Prophet's own bed as a decoy, fully aware of the danger, so the Prophet could slip away safely - an act of trust and courage the Prophet never forgot. He later married the Prophet's beloved daughter Fatimah, and their household became the source of the Prophet's own grandchildren, Hasan and Husayn.

Known for his deep knowledge and sharp judgment, Ali became the fourth Caliph during a period of great difficulty and internal conflict within the Muslim community, facing wars he had never sought. He was martyred while praying in the mosque of Kufa, struck by a poisoned sword from a extremist opponent. He remains remembered as a man of remarkable bravery in battle, humility in worship, and precision in matters of justice and Islamic law.
