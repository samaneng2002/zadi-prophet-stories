Among the Children of Israel was a coastal town whose people were commanded to keep the Sabbath as a day of rest and worship, refraining from work and, specifically, from fishing. Allah tested them by making the fish appear abundantly and openly on Sabbath days, while on other days they came rarely and hidden - a trial aimed precisely at the temptation they were most likely to face.

Rather than resist, some of the townspeople began devising tricks to catch fish on the Sabbath without technically "working" that day - digging channels or setting traps the day before so the fish would be trapped by Saturday, then collected afterward. They told themselves they were keeping the letter of the command while breaking its spirit entirely.

The town split into three groups: those who transgressed, those who stayed silently uninvolved, and a small group who openly warned the wrongdoers and rebuked them. When the punishment came, the warners were saved, and the transgressors were struck down - some accounts describe them being transformed, a stark lesson that Allah's commands are not loopholes to be gamed, and that silence in the face of open wrongdoing is not innocence.
