Sulayman (Solomon) commanded not only men and jinn but the birds as well, and one day noticed his hoopoe bird was missing. The hoopoe returned with news of a kingdom in Sheba (Saba) ruled by a queen, Bilqis, who possessed a magnificent throne but whose people worshipped the sun instead of Allah. Sulayman sent her a letter inviting her to submit to Allah alone.

Bilqis, wise and cautious, consulted her advisors before responding, then sent gifts to test Sulayman's intentions - a gesture he firmly rejected, making clear that what Allah had given him far exceeded any worldly gift, and that he sought only her submission to the truth. Realizing the seriousness of his message, she set out to visit him herself.

Before she arrived, Sulayman asked his court who could bring her throne to him, and a man with knowledge of the Scripture brought it in the blink of an eye through Allah's permission. Sulayman had it altered slightly and asked her if it resembled her own throne; she recognized it despite the changes. Led into his grand palace of glass and flowing water, she mistook the floor for water and lifted her garment - only to realize her mistake and, in that very moment, declare her full submission to Allah, Lord of all the worlds.
