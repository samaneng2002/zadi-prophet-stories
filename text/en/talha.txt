Talha ibn Ubaydullah was among the earliest converts to Islam, brought in through the same close friendship network as Abu Bakr, and he was one of the ten companions the Prophet personally promised Paradise to during their lifetime. He was known throughout Madinah for his exceptional generosity, regularly giving away large portions of his wealth to help those in need without hesitation.

His greatest moment of personal courage came at the Battle of Uhud, when a sudden collapse in the Muslim lines left the Prophet dangerously exposed to enemy fighters. Talha threw himself directly in front of the Prophet, absorbing blow after blow meant for him, and lost the use of a finger while shielding the Prophet's face with his own hand. By the battle's end, his body carried dozens of wounds from arrows and swords, all taken in the Prophet's defense.

The Prophet later said of that day that whoever wished to see a man walking the earth who had already secured his place in Paradise should look at Talha. He remained a respected and influential figure among the companions for the rest of his life, remembered above all for the moment he placed his own body between the Prophet and death.
