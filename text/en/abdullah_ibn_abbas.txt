Abdullah ibn Abbas was the Prophet's own first cousin, still a young boy when the Prophet passed away, yet he grew to become one of the most important scholars in all of Islamic history - a fact he attributed directly to a private supplication the Prophet once made specifically for him.

As a child, Ibn Abbas would prepare water for the Prophet's ablution before prayer, and one day the Prophet, noticing his eagerness to learn and serve, placed his hand on him and prayed: "O Allah, grant him deep understanding of the religion and teach him the true meaning of the Quran." From that moment, Ibn Abbas devoted himself entirely to seeking knowledge, and later described how he would personally visit the homes of older companions, waiting patiently at their doors even while they napped, rather than disturb them, simply to learn a single narration from them.

He became known as "Turjuman al-Qur'an," the Interpreter of the Quran, and his explanations of the Quran's meanings and reasons for revelation remain foundational to Quranic scholarship to this day, quoted constantly in tafsir literature across the centuries. Despite losing his eyesight later in life, he continued teaching and issuing religious rulings until his death, remembered as living proof that the Prophet's own supplication for him had been answered in full.
