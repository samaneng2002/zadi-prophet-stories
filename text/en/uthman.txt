Uthman ibn Affan was a wealthy, respected merchant from the noble Umayyad clan of Quraysh when he accepted Islam early on, a decision that cost him standing among his own tribe. He married two of the Prophet's own daughters in succession, Ruqayyah and then, after her death, Umm Kulthum, earning him the title "Possessor of the Two Lights."

He became famous for his extraordinary generosity, most notably when Madinah faced a severe water shortage and the only sweet well belonged to a Jewish man who charged the Muslims for every drop. Uthman purchased the well outright and made its water free for everyone, and separately equipped an entire army during a difficult expedition almost single-handedly from his own wealth, earning the Prophet's declaration that nothing Uthman did after that day could ever harm him.

As the third Caliph, Uthman is especially remembered for commissioning the compilation of the Quran into a single, standardized written text, distributed to every Muslim province to preserve it exactly as revealed - the very foundation of the Mus'haf still read today. He was killed by rebels while sitting at home reciting the Quran, refusing to let his own companions fight to defend him and choosing not to shed Muslim blood even to save his own life.
