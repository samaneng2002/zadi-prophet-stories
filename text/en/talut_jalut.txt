After the era of the Prophets Musa and Harun, the Children of Israel asked their Prophet to appoint a king for them so they could fight in Allah's cause against an oppressive enemy. Allah chose Talut (Saul) - a man of no great wealth or lineage, but blessed with knowledge and physical strength - and many of the people objected, thinking themselves more deserving.

As a sign confirming Talut's kingship, the sacred Ark was restored to them, carried by angels. When Talut led his army out, he tested them at a river, commanding that none drink from it except a small handful - only a few obeyed, and it was this small, disciplined group that went on to face the enemy, trusting that "how many a small group has overcome a large group by Allah's permission."

Among the enemy ranks stood Jalut (Goliath), a giant of a warrior none dared face. A young man named Dawud (David), still an ordinary soldier at the time, stepped forward and killed Jalut with a single stone from his sling. Allah later gave Dawud both kingship and prophethood - a reminder that true strength comes from faith and Allah's support, not numbers or size.
