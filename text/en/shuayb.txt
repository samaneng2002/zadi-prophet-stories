Shu'ayb (peace be upon him) was sent to the people of Madyan, a community known for their dishonesty in trade - cheating customers by shortchanging their measures and weights, and spreading corruption throughout the land. Shu'ayb called them to worship Allah alone and to deal with one another honestly, warning them that cheating people out of their due was among the gravest of wrongs.

His people mocked his concern for something as ordinary as fair trade, questioning why his prayer should tell them what to do with their own wealth, and threatened to expel him and his followers from their town unless they returned to the old ways. Shu'ayb responded that he sought nothing but to set things right, as far as he was able, and that his success came only through Allah.

When they persisted in denial and arrogance, a terrible, violent blast and an earthquake overtook them, and by morning they lay lifeless in their homes, as though they had never lived there at all. Shu'ayb and the believers were saved. His story is often remembered as the account of the Prophet whose call centered on honesty in everyday dealings - a reminder that faith and fair conduct in ordinary life are inseparable.
