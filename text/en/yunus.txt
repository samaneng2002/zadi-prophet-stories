Yunus (Jonah, peace be upon him) was sent to the people of Nineveh, and after calling them to Allah without success, he left the city in frustration before receiving Allah's permission to do so. He boarded a ship, which was later caught in a violent storm so severe the sailors began casting lots to lighten their load by throwing a passenger overboard - and the lot fell on Yunus three times.

He was cast into the sea, where a great fish swallowed him whole by Allah's command, not to destroy him but to preserve him. In the darkness of the fish's belly, within the darkness of the sea, within the darkness of the night, Yunus turned to Allah in humble supplication, declaring there is no god but Him, and glorifying Him, admitting he had been among the wrongdoers.

Allah answered his call and had the fish cast him onto the shore, weak and unwell, where a plant grew to shade him as he recovered. He was then sent back to his people, who - remarkably, unlike so many nations before them - believed as a whole once they saw the punishment they had been warned of drawing near, and Allah lifted it from them. Yunus's story is remembered as a lesson in never losing hope in Allah's mercy, no matter how dark the moment.
