Musa (Moses, peace be upon him) was born at a time when Pharaoh, fearing a prophecy that a child of the Israelites would bring about his downfall, ordered every newborn son among them to be killed. Allah inspired Musa's mother to place her infant in a basket on the river, and he was found and raised, remarkably, within Pharaoh's own household - his sister watching from a distance, and his own mother later brought in to nurse him without Pharaoh's family ever realizing who she was.

As a young man, Musa fled Egypt after accidentally killing a man in a scuffle, and made his way to Madyan, where he assisted two women in watering their flock and was taken in by their father, eventually marrying one of his daughters. Years later, traveling back toward Egypt with his family, Musa saw a fire on the mountainside and went to investigate - only to be addressed directly by Allah from a tree, commanded to remove his sandals in that sacred valley, and given his mission: to go to Pharaoh, who had transgressed all bounds, and call him back to Allah.

Musa returned to Egypt with his brother Harun at his side, and the two confronted Pharaoh with clear signs - his staff turning into a serpent, and his hand shining bright without any harm. Pharaoh's magicians, brought in to discredit him, instead recognized the truth the moment their own tricks were overcome, and fell down in prostration despite Pharaoh's fury. Plague after plague struck Egypt, yet Pharaoh's heart remained hardened, until Musa was commanded to lead the believers out by night.

Pharaoh pursued them to the shore of the sea, certain he had trapped them, but Allah commanded Musa to strike the sea with his staff, and it parted into two towering walls of water with a dry path between them. The believers crossed safely, and when Pharaoh's army followed onto that same path, the sea closed back over them, and Pharaoh drowned at the very moment he finally declared his belief - too late for it to be accepted. Musa went on to receive the Torah, and though his people wavered - even worshipping a golden calf in his brief absence - he continued to guide them with patience through decades of trial in the wilderness, standing as the Prophet most often mentioned by name in the Quran.
