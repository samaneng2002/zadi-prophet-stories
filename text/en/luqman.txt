Luqman was a wise man, not a Prophet, whom Allah blessed with wisdom rather than wealth or worldly status - a wisdom that mattered more to him than anything the world could offer. The Quran preserves his advice to his own son as a lasting lesson for every parent and child.

Luqman began with the foundation of everything else: "O my son, do not associate anything with Allah, for association is a great injustice." From there he taught that even something as tiny and hidden as a mustard seed inside a rock, known only to Allah, is fully within His knowledge - so nothing anyone does, however small or secret, is ever truly hidden.

He then moved to practical guidance: establish prayer, enjoin what is right, forbid what is wrong, and bear patiently whatever befalls you as a result, for this is a matter requiring real resolve. He warned against turning one's cheek away from people out of arrogance or walking through the earth exuberantly, and taught moderation in one's walk and voice - reminding his son that the harshest of all sounds is the braying of a donkey. In a few short verses, Luqman's advice covers belief, worship, character, and humility - a complete guide condensed into a father's words to his son.
