Abu Bakr As-Siddiq was the Prophet Muhammad's closest friend from before Islam, and the very first adult man to accept it, believing him instantly and without a single question when others hesitated. His honesty earned him the title "As-Siddiq" - the Truthful One - after he affirmed the Prophet's Night Journey to Jerusalem and back in one night, when even many believers doubted it.

He spent his considerable wealth freely to free enslaved Muslims being tortured for their faith, including Bilal ibn Rabah, and stood as the Prophet's sole companion during the dangerous migration to Madinah, sheltering with him in the Cave of Thawr while their pursuers stood just outside. The Prophet trusted him so completely that he chose him to lead the prayer in his final illness.

When the Prophet passed away and many Muslims were shaken with grief and disbelief, Abu Bakr stood before them and said the words that steadied the entire community: "Whoever worshipped Muhammad, then Muhammad has died; but whoever worshipped Allah, then Allah is alive and does not die." He became the first Caliph, holding the young Muslim community together through the difficult wars of apostasy, and remains remembered as the most beloved companion to the Prophet after his own family.
