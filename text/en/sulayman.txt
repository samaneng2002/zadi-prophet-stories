Sulayman (Solomon, peace be upon him), son of Dawud, inherited both prophethood and a kingdom unlike any given to anyone before or after him. Allah granted him authority over the wind, which carried him swiftly wherever he wished, dominion over the jinn who worked for him building structures and diving for treasures, and the remarkable ability to understand the speech of birds and other creatures.

Once, marching with his army, Sulayman heard an ant warn its fellow ants to enter their homes so as not to be crushed unknowingly - and he smiled at hearing it, grateful to Allah for such a gift of understanding. On another occasion, a hoopoe bird brought him news of the Queen of Sheba and her people, who worshipped the sun instead of Allah, and Sulayman sent her a letter inviting her to submit to Allah, arranging for her magnificent throne to be brought to his court before she even arrived, as a sign of the power Allah had granted him.

The Queen, upon witnessing these signs and touring his glass palace floor that she mistook for water, recognized the truth and submitted to Allah alongside Sulayman. Despite ruling over such vast wealth and power, Sulayman remained humble, frequently asking Allah to keep him grateful for these blessings rather than let them become a source of pride - a balance of immense capability paired with sincere humility.
