Musa (Moses) once said, in reply to a question, that he was the most knowledgeable person on earth. Allah corrected him by pointing him toward a servant of His at the junction of two seas who possessed a kind of knowledge Musa did not have. Musa set out with his young companion to find him, using a fish they carried as a sign - wherever it came back to life and slipped away, that would be the place.

Musa found the man - known as Al-Khidr - and asked to accompany him and learn from him. Al-Khidr agreed, but warned that Musa would not be able to bear patiently what he was about to witness. True to the warning, Al-Khidr then did three things that seemed outright wrong: he damaged a ship, killed a young boy, and repaired a wall in a town that had refused them hospitality - and each time, Musa could not stay silent and questioned him.

At the end, Al-Khidr explained the hidden wisdom behind every action: the ship was damaged to save it from a king who was seizing every good boat by force; the boy would have grown into a source of grief for his righteous parents, and Allah replaced him with a better child; and the wall protected a treasure belonging to two orphans, meant to be uncovered only once they came of age. The story teaches that Allah's wisdom often lies far beneath what appears, on the surface, to be harsh or unjust.
