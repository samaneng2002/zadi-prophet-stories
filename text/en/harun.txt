Harun (Aaron, peace be upon him) was the elder brother of Musa, and when Musa was given his mission at the burning bush, he asked Allah to appoint Harun alongside him as a helper, since Harun was more eloquent in speech and could support him in conveying the message to Pharaoh. Allah granted this request, and the two brothers went to Egypt together, sharing the burden of confronting one of history's most powerful tyrants.

Harun stood beside Musa through the confrontations with Pharaoh's magicians, the plagues, and the flight from Egypt, offering steady support at every stage. When Musa went up the mountain to receive the Torah and left Harun in charge of the people, a man named Samiri fashioned a golden calf and convinced many of the Israelites to worship it in Musa's absence.

Harun warned his people forcefully against this, telling them clearly that the calf was only a trial and that their true Lord was Allah, the Most Merciful - yet many of them refused to listen and continued in their error until Musa returned. When Musa, angered at what he found, questioned Harun sharply, Harun explained gently that he had feared causing greater division among the people had he acted with force alone, and Musa accepted his brother's sincerity. Harun's story is remembered as one of loyal partnership and patient leadership under intense pressure.
