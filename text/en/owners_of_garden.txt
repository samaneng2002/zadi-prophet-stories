The Quran tells of a group of brothers who owned an orchard and, one night, swore among themselves to harvest all its fruit early the next morning before dawn - specifically so they could go without giving anything to the poor, as their righteous father had always done before them. They set off determined and confident, never once saying "if Allah wills."

When they arrived, they found the entire garden burnt and blackened, as if it had been picked completely clean overnight. At first, thinking they had simply lost their way, they soon recognized the truth: this was their garden, utterly destroyed as punishment. They turned on one another, blaming each other for the plan, until the most moderate among them reminded them: "Did I not tell you, why do you not exalt Allah?"

Recognizing their wrongdoing, they turned to Allah in genuine repentance, admitting they had indeed been transgressors, and hoping their Lord might replace it with something better. The Quran closes the story by noting that such is the punishment of this world, but the punishment of the Hereafter is even greater - if only people truly knew it. The story stands as a reminder that withholding charity out of greed, and planning without any regard for Allah's will, invites loss rather than the abundance one hoped to keep.
