Khadijah bint Khuwaylid was a successful, respected businesswoman in Makkah, known for her wealth and impeccable character, who hired the young Muhammad to manage a trading caravan to Syria. So impressed was she by his honesty and the extraordinary profit he returned with, that she proposed marriage to him herself, despite being older than him and having turned down many wealthy suitors from Quraysh before.

Their marriage became a partnership of deep love and trust, and Khadijah was the very first person Muhammad turned to after the shock of his first revelation on Mount Hira, trembling and afraid. She comforted him immediately, telling him Allah would never let him down, since he was honest, kind to relatives, and helped the poor - and she became the first human being ever to believe in his message.

She spent her entire fortune supporting the Prophet and the earliest Muslims through the years of the Makkan boycott, when the small community was cut off and starved of food and trade for openly following Islam. She passed away shortly before the migration to Madinah, in what the Prophet later called "the Year of Sorrow," and he continued speaking of her with love and gratitude for the rest of his life, once telling Aisha that no woman had ever been better for him than Khadijah.
