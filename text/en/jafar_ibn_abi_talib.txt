Ja'far ibn Abi Talib was Ali's older brother and the Prophet's own cousin, known for a gentleness and generosity so notable that the Prophet nicknamed him "Abu al-Masakin," Father of the Poor, for how he cared for those with nothing. When persecution against the small Muslim community in Makkah grew unbearable, the Prophet sent a group of believers, with Ja'far as their spokesman, to seek refuge across the sea in the Christian kingdom of Abyssinia.

There, when Quraysh sent envoys demanding the Negus hand the Muslims back, Ja'far stood before the king and delivered one of the most eloquent speeches in early Islamic history, describing their pagan past, their transformation through Islam, and reciting verses from Surah Maryam describing Isa (Jesus) and his mother with such reverence that the Negus wept and refused to surrender them, offering them full protection instead.

Years later, Ja'far returned to fight at the Battle of Mu'tah, where he took command after the first appointed leader fell in battle. He fought on with tremendous courage even after losing one arm and then the other to sword strikes, reportedly holding the Muslim banner between the stumps of his arms until he too was killed. The Prophet told the Muslims that Allah had given Ja'far two wings in Paradise to fly with in place of the arms he lost, and he became known ever after as "Ja'far at-Tayyar" - Ja'far the Flyer.
