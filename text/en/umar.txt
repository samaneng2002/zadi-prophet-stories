Umar ibn Al-Khattab was once one of Islam's fiercest opponents, a powerfully built man known for his temper, who set out one day intending to kill the Prophet himself. On his way, he learned his own sister and her husband had secretly embraced Islam, and turned toward their house in fury - only to hear them reciting the Quran, and to feel his heart soften as he read the verses himself.

He went straight to the Prophet and declared his faith, and his open, fearless entry into Islam was such a turning point that the Muslims, previously forced to pray in secret, could now pray openly at the Ka'bah for the first time. The Prophet had prayed for exactly this - that Allah would strengthen Islam with either Umar or another famous opponent - and it was Umar's conversion that answered that prayer.

As the second Caliph after Abu Bakr, Umar oversaw the rapid expansion of the Muslim state while remaining famously humble, patching his own clothes and walking the streets of Madinah at night to check on the needs of the poor with his own eyes rather than trusting reports alone. He was martyred while leading the dawn prayer, stabbed by an assassin, and is remembered as a ruler whose justice was so uncompromising that people said "if a mule stumbled in Iraq, Umar would fear being asked about it by Allah."
