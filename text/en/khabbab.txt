Khabbab ibn Al-Aratt was among the earliest converts to Islam, an enslaved blacksmith in Makkah with no powerful clan to shield him, which made him one of the most brutally persecuted of the early Muslims. His owner would drag him out onto burning coals and press hot iron against his back to force him to renounce his faith, and Khabbab bore the scars of that torture on his body for the rest of his life.

He once brought his suffering directly to the Prophet, asking him to pray for relief from the persecution. Rather than offering immediate comfort, the Prophet reminded him of believers before them who were sawn in half or combed with iron and still never abandoned their faith, teaching Khabbab that this trial, too, was part of a much larger test, and that Allah would surely complete His religion regardless of how long it took.

Khabbab lived to see that promise fulfilled, witnessing Islam's spread across Arabia and beyond in his own lifetime. Even in old age, he would show visitors the scars still marking his back, not out of complaint, but as living proof of what the earliest Muslims endured for their faith - a reminder that patience through hardship was itself a form of worship for the earliest generation of believers.
