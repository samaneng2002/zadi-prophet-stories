Ibrahim (Abraham, peace be upon him) grew up among a people, including his own father, who carved idols and worshipped them. Even as a young man, he questioned this openly, reasoning that a star, then the moon, then the sun could not truly be his Lord, since each one sets and disappears - only the One who created them all deserved worship.

To prove the powerlessness of the idols to his people, Ibrahim broke them all except the largest, hanging his axe on it, so that when his people returned they would be forced to confront the absurdity of blaming a stone statue for what only a person could do. Furious, they resolved to burn him alive in a great fire - yet Allah commanded the fire to become cool and safe for Ibrahim, and he emerged from it entirely unharmed.

Later in his life, Ibrahim was tested with the command to sacrifice his son Ismail, and both father and son submitted to Allah's will with full sincerity - only for Allah to replace the sacrifice with a ram at the final moment, honoring their submission without requiring the boy's life. Ibrahim, together with Ismail, later raised the foundations of the Kaaba in Makkah, and his unwavering devotion earned him the title "Khalilullah" - the close friend of Allah.
