Yusuf (Joseph, peace be upon him) was the beloved son of Yaqub, envied by his own brothers for the special place he held in their father's heart. Driven by jealousy, his brothers threw him into the depths of a well and returned to their father with a fabricated story, while Yusuf was found by travelers and sold into slavery in Egypt.

There, he was raised in the house of a nobleman, growing into a man of striking character and integrity. When the nobleman's wife attempted to seduce him, Yusuf refused firmly, choosing imprisonment over sin, and spent years in prison where he interpreted the dreams of fellow inmates and, eventually, the dream of the king himself - foreseeing seven years of abundance followed by seven years of famine.

His wise counsel led to his release and his appointment over the treasuries of Egypt, where he managed the years of famine with such foresight that his own brothers, unaware of his identity, eventually came seeking grain and stood before him. Yusuf forgave them completely, saying there would be no blame upon them that day, and reunited with his father Yaqub, whose eyesight was restored the moment Yusuf's shirt touched his face. His story is remembered as the finest of accounts - one of patience, forgiveness, and unwavering trust that Allah's plan unfolds even through the darkest trials.
