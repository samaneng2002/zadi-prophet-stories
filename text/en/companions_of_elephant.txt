Abraha, the ruler of Yemen, built a magnificent cathedral in San'a and wanted the Arabs to make pilgrimage to it instead of the Ka'bah in Makkah. When an Arab desecrated the cathedral in anger at this attempt to replace the Ka'bah, Abraha swore to march on Makkah and destroy it, bringing with him a huge army led by an elephant, a beast the Arabs had never seen before - which gave the year its name, "the Year of the Elephant."

As Abraha's army approached, the elephant leading them - named Mahmud - knelt down and refused to move toward Makkah, no matter how it was struck or driven, while it would happily move in any other direction. Abdul Muttalib, the Prophet Muhammad's grandfather and then the Ka'bah's custodian, withdrew the townspeople to the surrounding mountains and simply prayed to Allah to protect His own House, placing no army between Abraha and the Ka'bah at all.

Allah then sent flocks of birds, each carrying small stones of baked clay, striking down the invaders one by one until they were left "like eaten straw" - a devastated army destroyed without a single Meccan lifting a weapon. This event took place in the very year the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) was born, and it stands as one of the clearest proofs of Allah's direct protection over His sacred House.
